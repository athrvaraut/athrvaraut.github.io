Shalimar Ops · Shalimar Nutrients · Apr – Jul 2026

Replacing a plant's handwritten logbooks

Shalimar Nutrients processes soya near Nagpur. Its boiler and power departments run three shifts a day, every day of the year. Operators wrote readings into logbooks by hand, and the head of department retyped them into Excel at the end of each month.

Shift A07:00 – 15:00
Shift B15:00 – 23:00
Shift C23:00 – 07:00

What I built

  • Phone-friendly forms for 20+ boiler readings per entry: steam pressure and flow, drum level, bed and flue-gas temperatures, fan dampers, coal feed. Out-of-range values warn the operator, for example steam pressure below 6 or above 12 kg/cm².
  • One entry per shift slot, with an "Update entry" option for corrections. Each day's opening coal stock is carried over from the previous day's closing stock.
  • A power report by plant section in kWh, with power factor calculated as MSEDCL kWh ÷ kVAh, and the share saved by solar.
  • An Excel engine that produces the daily boiler report, the 14-column monthly coal report and the monthly power report, laid out exactly like the plant's existing sheets.
  • Three roles (operator, assistant, head of department) with JWT login.
What I learned

Most of the project's 104 commits are fixes to the Excel output: merged cells that corrupted files, formulas, column widths and colours that had to match the paper format. People adopt a tool when its output looks like what they already trust. Matching their format mattered more than any new feature.

Facial Expression Recognition · ML course project · Oct 2026

Finding out why a model scored 54%

The project recognises seven facial expressions from a webcam in real time. Version 1 scored 54.05% on the FER-2013 test set. Before changing anything, I measured where it was failing. Every change in version 2 fixes one measured problem.

Measured problem in version 1Fix in version 2
568 of the 7,178 test images (7.9%) also appeared in training, and the test set doubled as the validation set.Official split: train, PublicTest for validation, PrivateTest used once. 1,798 duplicate, blank, conflicting or leaked training images removed.
Webcam faces were histogram-equalised but training images were not. Cost: 4.3 accuracy points.One preprocessing function shared by training and the live app, guarded by a unit test.
Heavy augmentation left the model underfitting: 55.2% even on its own training images.Moderate augmentation plus grey cutout for partly covered faces.
"Disgust" was recognised 0% of the time.Square-root class weights, and macro-F1 reported next to accuracy.
The Haar face detector found 72.9% of faces.YuNet detector: 96.7%, with eye alignment.
About 80 ms per face.Compiled inference: about 10 ms per face, about 29 FPS live.

How I build

I build with AI coding agents, mainly Claude Code, and I run them the way a lead runs a small team. The agent writes most of the code. I decide what gets built, how it should behave, and whether it is done.

  1. Spec first

    Before any code, I write down what the system must do, with every rule marked decided, proposed or open. The agent builds from decisions, not guesses.

  2. Small phases

    Each phase is one task and one commit, reviewed before the next one starts. Small diffs are where I catch mistakes.

  3. Tests decide when it's done

    A phase is finished when its tests pass and have actually been run, not when the code looks right.

  4. Measure before claiming

    Numbers come from runs, not estimates. That habit found the leaked test images in the FER project and the overfit strategy in the trading engine.
